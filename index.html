Sternum is not a medical device. It can't diagnose anything, and it may not notice a condition you already have, such as atrial fibrillation, a pacemaker rhythm or a valve problem. A "normal" result never rules one out. If irregular rhythm keeps showing up, check it with an ECG (Apple Watch ECG app, KardiaMobile, or your doctor).

How to take a good reading

  1. Same time each day, ideally soon after waking, before coffee. Lie flat on your back on a bed, with one thin pillow at most, and rest for 2 to 3 minutes before you tap Start, so your heart rate settles.
  2. Take the phone out of its case and lay it screen up, flat and lengthwise along the lower half of your breastbone (the flat bone in the middle of your chest), with its bottom edge toward your chin. Then a glance down shows the countdown and your heart rate the right way up.
  3. Arms by your sides. Breathe normally, don't talk, keep still. Do Not Disturb stops a buzz from spoiling the reading.
  4. Tap Start, settle during the 10-second countdown, and stay still until the closing chime. 60 seconds is enough; 2 minutes gives steadier HRV.
  5. Tag anything unusual (coffee, alcohol, short sleep, workout, feeling unwell). Tags explain outliers later.
  6. Mark 3–5 calm, typical mornings as your baseline. Until you do, the first three good readings are used.

What the signals can tell you

  • Heart rate and beat timing. Each beat jolts the chest (seismocardiography, from the accelerometer) and rocks it slightly (gyrocardiography, from the gyroscope). Sternum combines both, leaning on whichever shows the beat more clearly.
  • Heart rate variability (RMSSD). How much consecutive beats differ. Higher than your usual generally means rested; a sharp drop often follows poor sleep, alcohol, hard training or the start of an illness.
  • Breathing rate, regularity and pauses. The phone tilts as your chest rises, so the gravity reading traces each breath.
  • Breath-linked heart swing. Your heart speeds up on the in-breath and slows on the out-breath (respiratory sinus arrhythmia). A healthy, relaxed system shows a clear swing.
  • Rhythm regularity and extra beats. Large jumps between consecutive beats, and early beats followed by a pause, show up in the beat-interval chart and the Poincaré plot. A steady rhythm makes a tight comet shape; an irregular one scatters.
  • Beat shape. The averaged chest-motion waveform has peaks linked to valves opening and closing. Research groups use it for cardiac timing and strength. Here it is compared against your baseline shape; most changes come from phone placement.

What it cannot do: blood pressure, oxygen, an electrical ECG, or heart sounds and murmurs. It never uses the microphone.

What the numbers mean

Heart rate
Beats per minute while lying still. 50 to 100 is normal; fit people often sit in the 40s or 50s. A rise of 5 to 10 over your usual for a few days is the most common sign of illness, poor sleep or alcohol.
HRV
How much the gap between beats varies. Higher usually means rested and relaxed. It is very personal and drops with age, so only compare it with your own readings, and trust a trend over several days more than one low day.
Breathing
Breaths per minute. 12 to 20 is normal when awake; 8 to 12 is common lying relaxed. Over 20 at rest is fast.
Rhythm
Your heart speeds up a little when you breathe in and slows when you breathe out. That is normal. Sternum looks for gaps that jump around at random instead, which is what atrial fibrillation looks like.
Breath swing
How much your heart rate rises and falls within each breath. A clear swing (5+ bpm) means a relaxed, responsive nervous system; it gets smaller with age, stress and faster breathing.
Early beats
A beat that comes early, then a longer pause. Nearly everyone has some. Lots of them, or ones you feel with dizziness, are worth a check.

Slow breathing makes both HRV and breath swing bigger, so compare readings taken the same way.

Signs it can graph

These are wellness flags measured against your own baseline, not diagnoses.

Resting heart rate up
Short sleep, alcohol, dehydration, stress, oncoming illness or fever
Shows when: 8+ bpm above baseline, or over 100
HRV down
Same as above; often the earliest sign of being run down
Shows when: About a third below baseline
Breathing faster
Illness, anxiety, fever, lung or heart strain
Shows when: 3+ breaths/min above baseline, or over 20
Irregular rhythm
Beat gaps that jump around at random. Can be atrial fibrillation; movement and extra beats can mimic it
Shows when: Big, random changes between beats in a clean recording of 40+ beats. The normal rise and fall with breathing is ignored
Extra beats
Premature beats; common with caffeine, stress, fatigue
Shows when: 3 or more in a reading
Long gap between beats
Usually the phone losing a beat; occasionally a real pause
Shows when: A gap over 2 s
Breathing pause
Breath-holding or a pause in breathing
Shows when: No breathing motion for 12 s or more
Beat shape changed
Usually a different phone position; occasionally a real change
Shows when: Low match with the baseline waveform

Who else is doing this

  • University of Turku and Precordior (Finland) built smartphone mechanocardiography: a phone on the chest, accelerometer plus gyroscope, machine learning on top. Their 2018 study reported about 98% accuracy for detecting atrial fibrillation (Iftikhar et al., Scientific Reports).
  • CardioSignal is the resulting app, aimed at detecting heart conditions from a one-minute phone-on-chest reading, EU-funded (CORDIS project).
  • REFLECS heart-failure study: 1,003 people, a phone on the sternum for 2 minutes; it recognised heart failure with an AUC of 0.95, and the gyroscope beat the accelerometer (JACC: Heart Failure, 2024; trial record).
  • Related consumer devices: digital stethoscopes such as Eko analyse heart sounds; smartwatches flag irregular rhythm and breathing disturbances from wrist sensors.

How your data is kept

Everything stays on this phone. Raw motion data from your last 10 readings is kept so you can share it in a chat with Claude; older raw data is dropped automatically, but the numbers and charts stay. Use Sessions → Save file to keep a backup, or attach the file to a chat with Claude to talk through your readings.

Sternum makes no requests to other sites, and after the first visit it opens without a connection. Clearing Safari's website data deletes your readings, and Safari may clear them itself if you don't open the site for 7 days, unless it's on your Home Screen.
